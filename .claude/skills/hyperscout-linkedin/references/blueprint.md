What it says:

1. News with a famous name buys reach (strangers), not reactions.
2. People and places buy reactions from the network.
3. Buyer's-chair stories are the steadiest trust builders.
4. Abstract wisdom flops: same message, no name, no number, no story, under 470 impressions.
5. Asking for reposts works.
6. More than 2 posts a week dilutes reach.
7. Closing questions are too broad; ask for one specific experience.

## Jan's voice at its best

- First line is a fact or a confession, with a number.
- Short sentences, one idea per paragraph, real white space.
- The turn: what everyone believes, then what is true. "For years I told myself the problem was the product. It wasn't."
- Fair, never bitter. Criticise the system, never people.
- A closing line people remember. "Fair deals compound. Squeezed deals expire."
- The insider's eye: buyer for his mother's store, buyer in India, his own brand, the shop floor.

## Where the voice slips

- Tech and investor language: "operating layer", "plumbing", "AI-powered sourcing at scale", "game-changer".
- Press-quote lists and "amazing to see the support" posts.
- Hashtag walls and 10 tagged names at the bottom.
- Typos. Read once out loud.
- Questions too wide to answer.

## The four formats

| Format | Does | Benchmark | How often | Recipe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Famous name, hard number | Reach | 1,750 to 11,000 impressions | 1 a week | Known brand or retailer, one number, the turn, what it means for a brand owner, one narrow question. Source in first comment. |
| 2. Buyer's chair | Trust, comments | about 1,000 impressions, 20 to 27 reactions | Every other week | Confession with a number, what Jan did, what it taught him, a closing line. No pitch. |
| 3. On the floor | Network reactions | 1,250 to 2,400 impressions, 46 to 63 reactions | Around every show | Photo, one feeling, 3 to 5 people named in the text, where we are next. |
| 4. Milestone and ask | Reposts, pipeline | 3,564 impressions, 14 reposts | Once a quarter | What happened in numbers, thank people, one clear ask. |

## Checklist

- First line has a name or a number and works on its own
- One idea; 80 to 200 words (stories may run longer)
- The turn is there
- About the brand's problem, not Hyperscout's technology
- Ends with a quotable line and one narrow question, or a clear ask
- 3 to 5 tags in the text at most, 0 to 2 hashtags, no em dashes, links in first comment
- Facts checked, source ready
- Read aloud once
- Not more than 2 posts this week

## Page and team

The Hyperscout page reposts Jan's format 1 and 3 posts with one line of its own and posts format 4 news. Team members post format 2 and 3 in the same style: Richard from the shows he visits, Sara from her calls with brands.
